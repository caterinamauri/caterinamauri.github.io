About

Language takes shape between people.

I study how the grammars of the world’s languages take shape in use: how interaction and the need to understand one another shape the ways speakers build categories, connect ideas, manage knowledge and expectations, and make meaning together.

I follow questions where they lead. A connective may open a path into cognition; a conversational fragment may challenge a typological generalisation; a corpus problem may suggest a new way of sharing linguistic data. The path is exploratory, but the landscape remains precise: diversity, change, interaction and cooperation.

Research

Current directions

01

Grammar and interaction

How grammatical patterns emerge through turn-by-turn coordination, shared attention and the continuous negotiation of meaning.

02

Typology in use

How languages organise alternatives, categories, knowledge and expectations—and how discourse data can reshape cross-linguistic comparison.

03

Resources as research

Open, modular and incremental corpora that preserve variation and make methodological choices visible and reusable.
