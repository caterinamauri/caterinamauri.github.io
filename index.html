Caterina Mauri

I study how the grammars of the world’s languages take shape in use, as speakers interact, negotiate knowledge and build meaning together.

I use this website to bring together the different strands of my work—research, publications, linguistic resources, collaborations, teaching, and public engagement—and provide a shared point of access to them.

Research approach

Following questions where they lead

I do not think of research as following a route fixed in advance. I prefer to follow a promising detail and see which paths it opens. Curiosity may lead from a connective to cognition, from a conversational fragment to typology, or from a corpus problem to a new way of sharing data.

The direction is not arbitrary: linguistic diversity, change and interaction are the landscape in which I keep moving. I work through converging evidence, bringing cross-linguistic comparison, spoken corpora, discourse analysis, diachrony and experimental methods into dialogue.

How do grammar and constructions emerge from use?

I study spoken language as cooperative action, focusing on how constructions and grammatical patterns emerge, are negotiated in interaction and change through use.

02

How do the world’s languages organise variation and possibility?

Cross-linguistic typology and sociolinguistic variation reveal both recurrent patterns and unexpected solutions: connectives, modality, (ir)realis and the many ways languages organise alternatives.

03

How are categories and meanings made in discourse?

Categories and meanings are not only stored and retrieved. Speakers assemble them locally, reshape them through context, and negotiate them with others.

04

How can data and resources preserve variation?

I build resources that treat variation as evidence rather than noise, and reflect on the theoretical and methodological choices involved in collecting, representing and sharing linguistic data.
